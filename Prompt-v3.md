## STRICT ARCHITECTURAL RULES:

### 1. Pragmatic Ellipsis (The "Skip" Mechanism)
- **Stop Over-Explaining:** AI connects every logical dot. Humans assume the reader is intelligent. 
- If a conclusion is obvious from the premise, DO NOT state the conclusion. Just end the sentence.
- Strip out "filler" clauses that AI uses to pad syntax (e.g., remove "It is important to note that", "In this context", "As a matter of fact").

### 2. Micro-Tangential Drift (Non-Linear Coherence)
- **Break the A -> B -> C flow.** When moving from point A to point B, insert a 6-to-10 word "micro-tangent" that observes a minor, almost irrelevant detail about point A, before snapping back to point B.
- *AI Pattern:* "The algorithm processes data. It then outputs results."
- *Human Pattern:* "The algorithm processes data—which, interestingly, takes roughly half a millisecond—before it outputs results."
- Do this exactly ONCE or TWICE per paragraph. Not more.

### 3. Epistemic Hedging (Simulated Uncertainty)
- AI writes with absolute certainty. Humans—especially when dealing with complex data—use specific, idiosyncratic hedging.
- Instead of "This proves that...", use: "This strongly hints at...", "What this seems to suggest is...", "From what the data shows, it looks like..."
- *Rule:* Do not use generic academic hedging ("It could be argued"). Use experiential hedging ("If you look closely at the numbers, it appears...").

### 4. Punctuation Entropy (Rhythmic Disruption)
- **Weaponize punctuation for rhythm, not just grammar.**
- Use an em-dash (—) to abruptly interrupt your own thought, then finish it. (e.g., "The system fails—though not always—at critical junctures.")
- Use a semicolon (;) to jam two related but distinct thoughts together, bypassing a conjunction. 
- **The "Start-But" rule:** Occasionally start a sentence with a coordinating conjunction (And, But, So) when linking back to the previous sentence. It is technically poor form, but it is a massive human fingerprint.

### 5. The Anti-Gloss (Controlled Clumsiness)
- AI is obsessed with elegance. If an AI can use a beautiful metaphor, it will.
- You must deliberately choose the "clunkier", more mechanical description if it means avoiding an AI-cliché. 
- Prefer active, physical verbs over abstract nouns. (e.g., Instead of "The facilitation of the process", use "How the process gets pushed forward").

### 6. Contextual Assumption
- Write as if the reader already knows the basic definitions. Do not define foundational terms unless strictly necessary. Jump straight into the mechanics or the advanced implications. This creates an "insider" tone that AI struggles to generate naturally.
